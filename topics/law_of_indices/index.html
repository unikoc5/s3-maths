Part 1 · What does a power mean?

1A. Powers: square and cube

Use the arrows. The index counts how many identical factors you multiply.

Step 1 / 3

Square = two identical factors

\(3^{2}=3\times 3\)
\(x^{2}=x\times x\)
Step 2 / 3

Cube = three identical factors

\(2^{3}=2\times 2\times 2\)
\(x^{3}=x\times x\times x\)
Step 3 / 3

General rule

\(x^{n}\) means \(n\) factors of \(x\) multiplied together.

\[ x^{n}=\underbrace{x\times x\times\cdots\times x}_{n\text{ times}} \]

1B. Why \(2x\) is not \(x^{2}\)

Addition changes the coefficient. A power is repeated multiplication.

Step 1 / 4

Square means two identical numbers multiplied

\[ a^{2}=a\times a \]

Step 2 / 4

Number examples

\(3\times 3=9=3^{2}\)
\(5\times 5=25=5^{2}\)
Step 3 / 4

If someone claims \(x\times x=2x\)

Put \(x=3\): then \(x\times x\) is \(3\times 3=9\), but \(2x\) is \(2\times 3=6\). That would force \(3\times 3=6\) — impossible.

\[ \text{If }x\times x=2x\text{ and }x=3,\text{ then }3\times 3=6\text{?} \]

Step 4 / 4

Conclusion

\(3\times 3=9=3^{2}\), so \(x\times x=x^{2}\). Addition is different: \(x+x=2x\).

\[ x\times x=x^{2}\qquad\text{and}\qquad x+x=2x \]

5. Roots / fractional indices (divide indices) · \(x^{\frac{n}{m}}=\sqrt[m]{x^{n}}\)

Step 1 / 4

What is a square root?

A square root undoes squaring — it is the inverse of the 2nd power.

\[ \left(\sqrt{a}\right)^{2}=a \]

Step 2 / 4

Higher roots

An \(m\)-th root undoes an \(m\)-th power: \(\left(\sqrt[m]{a}\right)^{m}=a\).

\[ \sqrt[m]{a^{m}}=a \]

Step 3 / 4

Why a fraction?

We want \(r\) with \(\left(x^{r}\right)^{m}=x\). Then \(x^{rm}=x^{1}\), so \(r=\dfrac{1}{m}\).

\[ x^{\frac{1}{m}}=\sqrt[m]{x} \]

Step 4 / 4

General rule

\(x^{\frac{n}{m}}=\left(x^{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{m}}=\sqrt[m]{x^{n}}\) (also equal to \(\left(\sqrt[m]{x}\right)^{n}\)).

\[ x^{\frac{n}{m}}=\sqrt[m]{x^{n}} \]

\[ x^{\frac{n}{m}}=\sqrt[m]{x^{n}} \]

6. Zero exponent · \(x^{0}=1\)

Step 1 / 4

Start from \(0=n-n\)

Any number minus itself is zero. Write the zero index as a difference of equal indices.

\[ 0=n-n \]

Step 2 / 4

Apply the quotient rule

Then \(x^{0}=x^{n-n}=\dfrac{x^{n}}{x^{n}}\). Same number on top and bottom cancels to \(1\) (for \(x\neq 0\)).

\[ x^{0}=x^{n-n}=\dfrac{x^{n}}{x^{n}}=1 \]

Step 3 / 4

Therefore \(0^{0}\) is undefined

Any positive power of \(0\) is \(0\): \(0^{n}=0\). The same pattern would become \(\dfrac{0^{n}}{0^{n}}\) — dividing by zero, which is not allowed.

\[ 0^{0}\text{ is undefined} \]

Step 4 / 4

Summary

Zero exponent is \(1\) only when the base is not zero.

\[ x^{0}=1\quad(x\neq 0),\qquad 0^{0}\text{ undefined} \]

\[ x^{0}=1\quad(x\neq 0),\qquad 0^{0}\text{ undefined} \]

7. Negative exponent · \(x^{-n}=\dfrac{1}{x^{n}}\)

Uses the quotient rule and \(x^{0}=1\).

Step 1 / 4

Proof · via division

Consider \(\dfrac{x^{0}}{x^{n}}\). Left side is \(\dfrac{1}{x^{n}}\) because \(x^{0}=1\).

\[ \dfrac{x^{0}}{x^{n}}=\dfrac{1}{x^{n}} \]

Step 2 / 4

Quotient rule on the indices

Right side: \(x^{0-n}=x^{-n}\). The two sides are equal, so the negative power is a reciprocal.

\[ x^{-n}=\dfrac{1}{x^{n}} \]

Step 3 / 4

Check · via product

\(x^{n}\cdot x^{-n}=x^{n+(-n)}=x^{0}=1\). The number that multiplies \(x^{n}\) to give \(1\) is \(\dfrac{1}{x^{n}}\).

\[ x^{n}\cdot x^{-n}=1 \]

Step 4 / 4

Summary

A negative index means “one over the positive power” (base not zero).

\[ x^{-n}=\dfrac{1}{x^{n}}\quad(x\neq 0) \]

\[ x^{-n}=\frac{1}{x^{n}}\quad(x\neq 0) \]

Part 1 · Standard form

1. What is scientific notation?

Worksheet §1–10: express ordinary numbers in the form \(a \times 10^{n}\).

Step 1 / 5

Standard form

Scientific notation (standard form) writes a number as a coefficient times a power of ten.

\[ a \times 10^{n}\qquad\text{where}\quad 1 \le a < 10 \]

Step 2 / 5

Why \(102\,000\,000 = 1.02 \times 10^{8}\)?

Factor out powers of ten step by step:

\[ 102\,000\,000 = 102 \times 1\,000\,000 = 1.02 \times 100\,000\,000 \]

Powers of ten are named by how many zeros follow the \(1\): \(10^{2}=100\) (two zeros), \(10^{3}=1000\) (three zeros), … so in general \(10^{n}\) is \(1\) followed by \(n\) zeros. Here \(100\,000\,000\) has eight zeros, so \(100\,000\,000=10^{8}\). Therefore

\[ 102\,000\,000 = 1.02 \times 10^{8} \]

Step 3 / 5

Rule: left shift ↔ index \(+1\)

Moving the decimal point one place left multiplies by \(10\), so the power of ten goes up by \(1\). Count how many places you move left — that count is \(n\).

\(9\,410\,000\,000 = 9.41 \times 10^{9}\)
Step 4 / 5

Why \(0.000\,504 = 5.04 \times 10^{-4}\)?

Factor using a fraction of a power of ten:

\[ 0.000\,504 = \dfrac{5.04}{10\,000} = 5.04 \times \dfrac{1}{10\,000} \]

\(10\,000\) has four zeros, so \(10\,000=10^{4}\). A negative power means a reciprocal: \(10^{-4}=\dfrac{1}{10^{4}}=\dfrac{1}{10\,000}\). Therefore

\[ 0.000\,504 = 5.04 \times 10^{-4} \]

Rule: each right shift of the decimal divides by \(10\), so the index goes down by \(1\) (negative for small numbers).

Step 5 / 5

Same idea for any shift

Each left shift is one factor of \(10\) (index \(+1\)); each right shift divides by \(10\) (index \(-1\)). That is \(10^{m}\times 10^{n}=10^{m+n}\).

\[ 2.45 \times 10^{2} \times 10^{6} = 2.45 \times 10^{8} \]

Part 1 · Base 2 and place value

1. From indices to binary

Each binary digit is a coefficient times a power of \(2\).

Step 1 / 4

Denary expanded form

\(763_{(10)} = 7\times10^{2} + 6\times10^{1} + 3\times10^{0}\)
Step 2 / 4

Binary is base \(2\)

\(11_{(2)} = 1\times2^{1} + 1\times2^{0} = 3_{(10)}\)
\(100_{(2)} = 1\times2^{2} = 4_{(10)}\)
Step 3 / 4

Only digits \(0\) and \(1\)

Each place is either “on” (\(1\)) or “off” (\(0\)) — like light bulbs in a row.

Step 4 / 4

Place value of a digit

\[ \text{In } 10111_{(2)}, \text{ the } 0 \text{ has place value } 2^{3} \]
